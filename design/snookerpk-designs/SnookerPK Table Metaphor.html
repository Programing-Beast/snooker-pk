Readability trade-offs

What the felt-and-wood treatment costs, and the adjustments already baked into the right-hand column to keep text legible.

Holds up well

  • Player names & scores — warm-white (#F3EFE3) and pure-white on baize clear ~9–12:1 contrast. Bolded winner still reads instantly.
  • LIVE badge — the deepened pink fill (#E11D48) pops hard against green; arguably stronger here than on white.
  • Brass accents — champagne secondary on felt is the most premium combination in the whole system. Used for titles, the “Enter” button and frame score.

Costs & fixes

  • Muted/secondary text dropped below AA on raw felt, so it’s lifted to a lighter green-grey (#AECBB9, ~4.8:1). Don’t push metadata any dimmer.
  • Light-surface badge tints (Upcoming brass-tint, Completed grey) vanish on green — they’re swapped for solid fills here. Every tinted chip needs an on-felt variant.
  • Flag chips with white fields (ENG) blend into glare; a 1px white ring restores the edge. The green band of PAK sits close to the felt — the ring is doing real work.

Where to use it

  • Hero / feature moments — live banner, the marquee tournament card, an event landing header. The atmosphere earns its weight.
  • Not for dense lists. A full 64-line draw or a rankings table in felt-and-wood gets heavy and tiring — keep those clean-neutral.
  • Wood = decoration only. The rail never carries status or meaning; it’s a frame. Keep its thickness fixed so it doesn’t eat layout on mobile.
